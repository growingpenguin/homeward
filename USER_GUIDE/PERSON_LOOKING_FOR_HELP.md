# I'm looking for someone, or someone may be looking for me

[All guides](USER_GUIDE.md) · [Volunteer guide](VOLUNTEER.md) · [Lead guide](LEAD.md)

Go to the shelter desk. A volunteer uses Homeward with you. **You do not need a Homeward account, a phone or an ID number.** You do not have to operate the app yourself.

These pictures use made-up people in a test copy. The details on your slip will be different.

## “I want my family to know I'm here.”

Tell the volunteer a name or nickname. It does not have to be your legal name. Give other details only if you know them and want to share them.

Then choose what should happen if someone looks for you:

| Your choice | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Ask me first** | “Come and ask me before sharing anything.” This is the starting choice. |
| **Tell them where I am** | “A volunteer can share how to reach me.” It is still handled by staff, with safety checks. |
| **Keep me out of searches** | “Don't show me to people searching.” Shelter staff still have your record. |

![Ask the volunteer to explain any choice you are unsure about.](screenshots/03-privacy-choice.png)

If someone may hurt you, tell the volunteer privately and ask for a lead. You do not have to agree to contact.

## “I'm looking for my family.”

1. Tell the volunteer your name or nickname and who you are looking for.
2. Give any names or nicknames you remember. A rough age can help. It's okay if you don't know.
3. The volunteer may send a request. You can add a short message, such as **It is me. I am safe.** Keep your location out of that first message.
4. Take your slip. Keep it somewhere private.

A possible match does not prove it is the right person. It also does not mean they agreed to meet.

## Keep both parts of your slip

The slip has a **code** and a **PIN**. You need both when you come back. You can take it to any shelter using Homeward.

![Keep the code and PIN together, and keep them private.](screenshots/04-slip.png)

The volunteer can print it or help you write it down. The PIN is only shown when the slip is made. Don't use the code in this picture; it is just a test example.

If you lose your slip, ask for a lead. A replacement makes the old slip stop working. The current demo can only replace it once staff can open your existing record, so recovery may need the project maintainer's help.

## “Is there any news?”

1. Bring your slip to a shelter desk.
2. Give the volunteer the code and PIN privately.
3. They check it and turn the screen towards you.

You might see:

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| **They want to connect. Here's how…** | Read the plan with the volunteer and ask for help following it. |
| **They asked us to tell you they are safe.** | They chose to share that they are safe. No location is included. |
| **Your request is saved…** | There is no contact plan to share. You can come back and check again. |

![This answer shares that someone is safe, without saying where.](screenshots/10-safe-only.png)

The waiting message does not tell you whether someone was found or said no. That keeps their choice private.

When you finish, give the screen back to the volunteer. They should clear your check before helping the next person.

## “Someone is asking about me.”

The volunteer reads the request to you. You can say:

- **“Yes, I want to connect.”** Agree on the exact message or meeting plan first.
- **“Tell them I'm safe, but not where.”** Only that short safe message is shared.
- **“Not now.”** You can decide later.
- **“No.”** They are not told that you declined.

If a child is involved, a lead must handle the checks before contact is shared.

You can ask to change your details or privacy choice. Ask staff to update your existing record. You should not need a second registration just to fix a mistake.
